1. The project in a timeline

Almost a decade of co-design with people living with Parkinson’s

Professor Tiago Mestre, neurologist, and Professor Sylvie Grosjean, communication expert, have led a team of researchers to co-design a self-care digital companion with people living with Parkinson’s

They began with iCARE-PD, a multinational project on integrated care networks. Within it grew eCARE-PD, a digital companion for self-care, which is becoming PD-TIPS.AI, an AI-driven, bilingual conversational recommender of tailored care tips.

iCARE-PD→eCARE-PD→PD-TIPS.AI

2 · The problem

About 1 hour a year with a neurologist.
About 8,765 hours on your own.

Sara Riggare, who lives with Parkinson’s, sees her neurologist once or twice a year, for about 30 minutes. The rest of the year is self-care. The decisions that shape daily life with Parkinson’s are made at home, and most of that work is invisible to the care team.

Illustration and figures: Sara Riggare, riggare.se

3 · Why self-tracking matters

For Riggare, self-tracking is “the most powerful weapon” she has against Parkinson’s: noticing how an activity, a food or a treatment affects you shows what the neurologist cannot see. Participants in our studies describe the same gap from both sides of the consultation.

4 · The rationale

A technology for everyday self-care

People living with Parkinson’s are left to “do their homework on their own”. Clinicians want a short snapshot of the months between visits. eCARE-PD is designed for the 8,765 hours. It helps people to:

  1. Choose what matters todayand turn it into a goal they can act on
  2. Track as neededself-tracking that makes sense to the person doing it
  3. Get tailored care tipsand resources, instead of a generic list of symptoms
  4. See their journeya synthetic portrait to bring to the next appointment

Prototype iterations

Each version of the technology, who tested it and what we learned.

Evolution of the taxonomy

From a list of symptoms to intentions to act. The taxonomy is how eCARE-PD organises self-care priorities: it was regrouped, tested and re-sorted with participants at each iteration, and now serves as the data model of the conversational recommender.
